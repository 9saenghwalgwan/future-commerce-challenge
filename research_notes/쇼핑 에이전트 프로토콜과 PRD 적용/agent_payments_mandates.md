# Agent Payment Authorization Standards and Network Programs (status as of 2026-11-22)

Scope: how AP2, Visa Intelligent Commerce / Trusted Agent Protocol, Mastercard Agent Pay / Verifiable Intent, Amex ACE, Stripe Shared Payment Tokens, PayPal, Adyen, and x402/stablecoin rails let a human hand an AI agent limited purchasing authority (amount caps, merchant scope, time window, conditions); how consent is proven; how disputes and liability work; and adoption as of Nov 2026.

Labels used below: **[SPEC]** = read directly in a spec, docs, or repo during this session. **[PRESS]** = vendor press release or news report. **[SECONDARY]** = aggregator, blog, or vendor-marketing summary that was not checked against a primary source. **[INFERENCE]** = my own reasoning. All dates are as stated by the source. "Checked" means looked at on 2026-11-22.

## 1. AP2 (Agent Payments Protocol): mandate types, fields, human-present vs human-not-present, who signs, and 2026 status

### Takeaway
AP2 changed a lot between versions. v0.1 (2025-09-16) had three mandates: Intent, Cart, and Payment. v0.2 (2026-04-28) replaced them with two: a **Checkout Mandate** and a **Payment Mandate**. Each comes in an **open** form, which holds constraints the user signs ahead of time, and a **closed** form, which covers one specific transaction. Both are SD-JWT verifiable credentials. Typed constraints cover merchant allow-lists, acceptable SKUs, an amount range, a budget, recurrence, and an execution-date window, which together are close to a native encoding of "buy X only if total ≤ N at merchant M before date D." Google donated AP2 to the FIDO Alliance on 2026-04-29. On GitHub, v0.2 is still labeled a spec, with no tagged release and no PyPI package.

**v0.1 mandate fields (Sept 2025, still widely cited in articles)** [SPEC, read from the pre-v0.2 `mandate.py`]
- `IntentMandate` fields: `user_cart_confirmation_required` (bool), `natural_language_description` (str), `merchants` (optional list), `skus` (optional list), `requires_refundability` (optional bool, "If true, items must be refundable."), `intent_expiry` (ISO 8601). **v0.1 had no structured price-cap field.** Any price limit lived only in the natural-language description. — [AP2 v0.1 mandate.py](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/61f5de49f47d0517182d664bd05b7df1ff1f4e40/src/ap2/types/mandate.py)
- `CartMandate` = `contents` (`CartContents`: `id`, `user_cart_confirmation_required`, `payment_request` [a W3C PaymentRequest], `cart_expiry`, `merchant_name`) plus `merchant_authorization` (a base64url JWT signed by the merchant). — [same](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/61f5de49f47d0517182d664bd05b7df1ff1f4e40/src/ap2/types/mandate.py)
- `PaymentMandate` = `payment_mandate_contents` (`payment_mandate_id`, `payment_details_id`, `payment_details_total`, `payment_response`, `merchant_agent`, `timestamp`) plus `user_authorization` (the user's signed authorization). — [same](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/61f5de49f47d0517182d664bd05b7df1ff1f4e40/src/ap2/types/mandate.py)

**v0.2 model (current spec)** [SPEC]
- Five roles: **Shopping Agent (SA)**, **Credential Provider (CP)**, **Merchant (M)**, which is "responsible for the integrity of the inventory, pricing, and any merchant discounts", **Merchant Payment Processor (MPP)**, and **Trusted Surface (TS)**, "a UI surface that is trusted to get informed user consent". The Trusted Surface **MUST be non-agentic**. All validation "MUST happen in deterministic code regardless of whether the role is agentic or not." — [AP2 v0.2 specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md)
- The two mandate types are identified by `vct`: closed Checkout `mandate.checkout.1`, open Checkout `mandate.checkout.open.1`, closed Payment `mandate.payment.1`, open Payment `mandate.payment.open.1`. Mandates are SD-JWTs, and other verifiable digital credential formats are allowed as an extension. — [specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md); [checkout_mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/checkout_mandate.md); [payment_mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/payment_mandate.md)
- The **Checkout Mandate** is bound to a **merchant-signed Checkout JWT** (`checkout_jwt`) through `checkout_hash`. With UCP, the Checkout JWT "MUST be the Checkout object." The **Payment Mandate** is bound to the same Checkout through the hash of the Checkout JWT, which must use a non-deterministic signature scheme such as ECDSA. — [specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md); [checkout_mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/checkout_mandate.md)
- **Open Checkout Mandate constraints:** `checkout.allowed_merchants` (an `allowed` list of `{name, website}`) and `checkout.line_items` (`items[]`, each with `acceptable_items[]` by product `id` and a `quantity`, evaluated as a max-flow match. One open mandate cannot be split across several checkouts). — [checkout_mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/checkout_mandate.md)
- **Open Payment Mandate constraints:** `payment.amount_range` (`min`, `max`, `currency`, checked against the final `payment_amount`); `payment.budget` (`max`, `currency`, cumulative across uses); `payment.agent_recurrence` (`frequency` such as `MONTHLY`, `max_occurrences`); `payment.allowed_payees`; `payment.allowed_payment_instruments` (for example `{id, type:"card", description:"network ··· 1234"}` or UPI); `payment.allowed_pisps`; `payment.reference` (`conditional_transaction_id`, which ties the payment to an open Checkout Mandate); `payment.execution_date` (`not_before`, `not_after`). — [payment_mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/payment_mandate.md)
- **Modes.** In *Human Present (Direct)*, "The User directly sees the closed Checkout and approves it and its payment explicitly." The closed mandates are signed with the user's key (`user_sk`) on a Trusted Surface after authentication (for example biometric) and consent. In *Human Not Present (Autonomous)*, the user approves open mandates (constraints). The agent then signs closed mandates with **its own key**, which is bound into the open mandate through a `cnf` claim. "Verifiers of Mandates *always* receive a closed Payment and Checkout Mandate, regardless of the mode." — [specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md); [flows](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/flows.md)
- **Safety rules in autonomous mode.** It is RECOMMENDED to set the open mandate's `exp` "to the smallest value that will allow the Shopping Agent to complete the assigned task". The agent "MUST NOT present any subsequent open Payment or Checkout Mandates without receiving a rejection receipt from the previous one", which prevents double-spending one mandate. The agent must disclose only the constraint disclosures that are needed (selective disclosure for privacy). — [specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md)
- **Falling back to the human.** "A Human Not Present flow can be turned into a Human Present flow by the Merchant (or Credential Provider) returning an `unresolved_constraint` error and bringing the User back into the loop." Other defined errors are `invalid_credential`, `invalid_mandate`, and `mandates_not_supported`. — [flows](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/flows.md); [agent_authorization](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/agent_authorization.md)
- **Two trust models for delegation.** In the *User Credential* model, an external issuer guarantees the Trusted Surface, so one user credential can delegate to many agents, with delegation through OpenID4VP. In the *Trusted Agent Provider* model, the agent's provider is the trusted party and verifiers must trust each provider. — [agent_authorization](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/agent_authorization.md)
- **Receipts.** The Merchant returns a signed Checkout Receipt. The MPP returns a signed Payment Receipt to the SA, the CP, and, if applicable, the Network. — [specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md)
- **Out of scope in v0.2:** agent-to-agent delegation, and splitting one open mandate across several checkouts. — [specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md); [checkout_mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/checkout_mandate.md)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Many 2026 articles still describe the Intent/Cart/Payment trio from v0.1. A team pitching "AP2-style mandates" in Nov 2026 should use the v0.2 vocabulary: open/closed Checkout and Payment Mandates, and the Trusted Surface. They can mention the v0.1 terms as background.
- [INFERENCE] AP2 does not care which commerce protocol sits underneath (UCP is the default) or which payment instrument is used. Its core rule, that a non-deterministic agent may assemble but deterministic code must verify against user-signed constraints, carries over to any merchant flow, including screen-driven checkout on Coupang or Naver. The merchant-signed Checkout JWT does **not** carry over, because Coupang and Naver do not sign checkouts for third-party agents.
- [INFERENCE] With no commits on `main` after 2026-04-29 and no release or package, active development has probably moved into the FIDO working groups. Treat AP2 as a reference design, not a deployed rail in Korea.

### Gaps
- I did not find primary confirmation of named production AP2 deployments with volumes as of Nov 2026, or of FIDO working-group outputs or drafts after April 2026.
- I did not render the exact JSON schema for each constraint (`schemas/` is generated). The field names above come from the prose docs and examples.

## 2. Visa Intelligent Commerce / Trusted Agent Protocol, Mastercard Agent Pay / Verifiable Intent: tokens, authentication, spending controls, agent registration, launches, and APAC/Korea status

### Takeaway
Both networks run (a) **agent-specific network tokens** bound to the user's card, (b) **authentication** (passkeys or biometrics) to capture consent, (c) **agent identity** (Visa TAP uses signed HTTP messages; Mastercard has agent registration and Verifiable Intent), and (d) issuer readiness programs. Korea is concretely involved. Visa's "Agentic Ready" program has included 6 Korean issuers since 2026-04-30. Shinhan Card completed a live Mastercard agent-pay transaction in March 2026. Korean card companies are targeting agent-pay launches in H1 2027. As of Nov 2026 the networks are still in pilots and acceptance onboarding, not broad consumer rollout in Korea.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Both networks are converging on the AP2/FIDO pattern: a signed user intent ("open mandate") plus agent-bound tokens plus an audit record. Mastercard's Verifiable Intent and AP2 v0.2 share SD-JWT and very similar constraint vocabularies. "Pre-captured consumer intent" (the Mastercard/Antom coffee purchase) is a working example of human-not-present delegation.
- [INFERENCE] For a Korean team, the realistic window for network-backed agent payments from Korean issuers is **2027**, matching the PRD's split of L1 now and L2 on the roadmap. The team can honestly say the rails are in issuer pilots in Korea (Visa Agentic Ready with 6 issuers; Shinhan with Mastercard) and that L2 depends on them.

## 3. Other rails: American Express ACE, Stripe Shared Payment Tokens, PayPal, Adyen

### Takeaway
Stripe's **Shared Payment Token (SPT)** is the most concrete public example of scoped delegation in production. The agent issues a token to one seller profile, with `usage_limits[max_amount]`, `usage_limits[currency]`, and `usage_limits[expires_at]`, and the token can be revoked. It is available only in the US, Canada, and Europe. **Amex** stands out for adding an explicit consumer **Agent Purchase Protection** (US only). PayPal and Adyen offer merchant- and PSP-side agentic stacks. I found no source for a product called "Order Intents."

### Cited Findings
- [SPEC] Stripe SPT: the agent creates a `SharedPaymentIssuedToken` with `payment_method`, `seller_details[network_business_profile]`, `usage_limits[currency]`, `usage_limits[max_amount]`, and `usage_limits[expires_at]` (API version `2026-09-30.preview`). The agent can **revoke** the token at any time (`POST /v1/shared_payment/issued_tokens/{id}/revoke`). Statuses are `active`, `requires_action` (for example 3DS), and `deactivated` (consumed, expired, or revoked). Webhooks: `shared_payment.issued_token.used`, `.deactivated`, `.requires_action`, `.active`. Supported methods are cards, Link, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Klarna, and Affirm (limited). Stripe may use network tokens from **Mastercard Agent Pay and Visa Intelligent Commerce** on the user's behalf. For Affirm, "the buyer must be the one navigating and confirming." Countries: the US, Canada, and select European countries, **not Korea**. — [Stripe Docs: Shared payment tokens](https://docs.stripe.com/agentic-commerce/concepts/shared-payment-tokens)
- [PRESS] On 2026-03-03 Stripe added support in SPTs for Mastercard Agent Pay, Visa Intelligent Commerce, Affirm, and Klarna. — [Stripe blog](https://stripe.com/blog/supporting-additional-payment-methods-for-agentic-commerce)
- [PRESS] At Sessions 2026, Stripe announced agent payments over the **Machine Payments Protocol (MPP, co-authored with Tempo)** in stablecoins or fiat, using SPTs through PaymentIntents. — [Stripe Sessions 2026 recap](https://stripe.com/blog/everything-we-announced-at-sessions-2026)
- [SECONDARY] The Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP, OpenAI with Stripe) uses SPTs as its delegated-payment primitive. Stripe launched an "Agentic Commerce Suite" on 2025-12-11. — [eco.com ACP explainer](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14845478-acp-agentic-commerce-protocol-explained); [digitalapplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/stripe-bnpl-shared-payment-tokens-2026-agentic-checkout-guide)
- [PRESS] On 2026-04-14 Amex launched the **Agentic Commerce Experiences (ACE) developer kit**, covering agent registration, account enablement, intent intelligence, payment credentials, and cart context. At launch the account enablement, intent intelligence, and payment credentials specs were available, while agent registration and cart context were still in development. Amex also launched **Amex Agent Purchase Protection**, which covers eligible charges caused by an error of a **registered** agent, on **US** credit cards only. The cardmember must first seek a return from the merchant where possible, and the protection is in addition to normal dispute rights. Amex's example: the user asked for green shoes and the agent bought non-refundable red ones. — [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/04/14/american-express-agentic-commerce-developer-kit-purchase-protection/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/14/american-express-ai-payments-developers-purchase-protection/); [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/american-express-launches-ace-developer-kit-and-agent-purchase-protection); [Amex agentic commerce page](https://www.americanexpress.com/en-us/company/agentic-commerce/)
- [PRESS] Amex argues the kit reduces chargebacks because only verified agents receive credentials and cardmembers are authenticated before using agents. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/14/american-express-ai-payments-developers-purchase-protection/)
- [PRESS] PayPal launched agentic commerce services (agentic payments, catalog and order management) on 2025-10-28 and joined Visa Agentic Ready's acceptance phase (2026-11-17), citing "Visa agentic tokens". — [Shopware / PayPal release](https://shopware.com/en/press/press-releases/paypal-launches-agentic-commerce-services-to-power-ai-driven-shopping); [Visa press release](https://usa.visa.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.releaseId.22991.html)
- [PRESS] On 2026-06-16 Adyen launched **Adyen Agentic**, with Agentic Feed, Agentic Cart, and Agentic Payments, alongside early participants including Amex, Mastercard, Visa, and Salesforce. — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/payments/2026/adyen-debuts-agentic-solutions-for-enterprise-merchants/); [Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/adyen-readies-for-agentic-commerce-with-adyen-agentic/)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] SPT's three limits (one seller, one max amount, one expiry) plus revocation and usage webhooks are the minimum controls a payment party actually enforces today. A permission card for L2 should show at least these, plus revocation.
- [INFERENCE] Amex's protection sets a "registered agent" plus "return first" standard for consumer remedies. A Korean L2 pitch should say who covers an agent error (issuer, AIR, or the team) and in what order (merchant return first, then dispute).

### Gaps
- "Stripe Order Intents" was not found in any source checked, so I cannot say whether it exists or what it does.
- I found no Korean availability for SPT, ACP, Amex ACE, PayPal agentic services, or Adyen Agentic.

## 4. "Conditional purchase" support (e.g., buy only if total ≤ X KRW at merchant M before date Y)

### Takeaway
AP2 v0.2 covers this case almost one-to-one with machine-checkable constraints: `checkout.allowed_merchants` + `checkout.line_items` + `payment.amount_range` (max X, KRW) + `payment.execution_date` (`not_after` Y) + `payment.allowed_payment_instruments`, signed once by the user on a Trusted Surface. When a constraint cannot be verified, the agent must fall back to asking the human. Mastercard's Verifiable Intent and AP4M, and Stripe's SPT limits, offer subsets. What none of them encodes is the "personal real price" concept: issuer post-billing discounts, points, or after-purchase costs. The amount constraint applies to the amount actually charged.

### Cited Findings
- [SPEC] `payment.amount_range`: "The `payment_amount` property of the Payment Mandate MUST be within the range defined by `min` and `max`. The `currency` property ... MUST match." — [AP2 payment_mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/payment_mandate.md)
- [SPEC] `payment.execution_date`: "The `execution_date` of the Payment Mandate MUST be later than or equal to `not_before` (if present) and earlier than or equal to `not_after` (if present)." — [AP2 payment_mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/payment_mandate.md)
- [SPEC] Merchant scope (`checkout.allowed_merchants`, `payment.allowed_payees`) and item scope (`checkout.line_items` with `acceptable_items` IDs and `quantity`). Repeat buys use `payment.agent_recurrence` + `payment.budget`. — [AP2 checkout_mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/checkout_mandate.md); [payment_mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/payment_mandate.md)
- [SPEC] Verifiers evaluate each constraint in deterministic code. Unknown or unverifiable constraints return `unresolved_constraint`, which "MAY be used as a signal to fallback to either a directly approved closed Mandate, or other non-agentic flows." — [AP2 agent_authorization](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/agent_authorization.md)
- [SPEC] New constraint types can be added by defining a unique `type`, a schema (including which fields are selectively disclosable), and an evaluation algorithm. — [AP2 specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md)
- [SPEC] v0.1's IntentMandate had only `merchants`, `skus`, `requires_refundability`, and `intent_expiry`, with no structured price cap. — [AP2 v0.1 mandate.py](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/61f5de49f47d0517182d664bd05b7df1ff1f4e40/src/ap2/types/mandate.py)
- [SPEC] Stripe SPT enforces `max_amount`, `currency`, `expires_at`, and a single seller profile. — [Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/agentic-commerce/concepts/shared-payment-tokens)
- [PRESS] Mastercard AP4M enforces business-set purchase and spend limits "at the point of payment". The consumer example used "pre-captured consumer intent". — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/mastercard-and-ant-international-advance-trusted-agentic-commerce-in-asia-pacific-302943668.html)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The "≤ X KRW" in an L2 condition must name which number it applies to. The amount a network or PSP can enforce is the **charged amount** (after merchant coupons and shipping). Card **post-billing discounts (청구할인)**, point accrual, and installment-interest effects are not in `payment_amount`. The team's "real personal price" is therefore a richer number than any current mandate can enforce. A sound design is a two-part condition: (a) an enforceable cap on the charged amount, and (b) an agent-side check on "effective cost" that is logged but not claimed as network-enforced.
- [INFERENCE] Price changes and coupon expiry after the user approves are exactly why AP2 binds the closed mandate to a hash of the actual checkout and requires the closed amount to fall inside the open range. An L2 design should re-read the actual final checkout page right before acting and abort (falling back to L1) if anything drifts out of range.

### Gaps
- I found no network or PSP feature that enforces conditions on **effective** price (net of issuer discounts or points), and no Korean-language documentation of conditional-purchase mandates.

## 5. Disputes, chargebacks, and liability for agent-initiated transactions (rules as of Nov 2026)

- [INFERENCE] The team's L1 (the agent stops before the pay button and the user taps pay in the merchant's own checkout) keeps the transaction a normal cardholder-initiated purchase. No new liability question arises, which is a strong and honest answer to the "AI를 안심하고 맡길 수 있나요?" criterion.

## 8. Implications: mapping mandate concepts to the PRD's L0/L1/L2 delegation levels, permission card UI, and 4-layer explanation

### Takeaway
The PRD can use AP2 v0.2's open/closed mandate model as its vocabulary for delegation. **L1 = the human approves the closed mandate.** The agent finds the real final checkout, stops, and the user taps pay on the merchant's own surface, so no delegated payment authority is used. **L2 = the human not present acting within an open mandate.** The user signs typed constraints through AIR, the agent acts only when every constraint passes in deterministic code, and otherwise it falls back to L1. Pair this with SPT-style limits and revocation and an AP2-style receipt and audit trail, and the official Track A criterion ("AI에게 어디까지 맡길지, 사용자 확인과 결제 승인 방법") gets a standards-aligned answer.

### Cited Findings (anchors for the suggestions)
- [SPEC] Human Present means the user directly approves the closed Checkout and payment. Human Not Present means the user approves constraints and the agent signs closed mandates with a key bound via `cnf`. Fallback to the human runs through `unresolved_constraint`. — [AP2 specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md); [flows](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/flows.md)
- [SPEC] The Trusted Surface must be non-agentic. The Shopping Agent is assumed agentic and is "a potential attacker", so all verification runs in deterministic code. — [AP2 specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md)
- [SPEC] Constraint vocabulary: `checkout.allowed_merchants`, `checkout.line_items`, `payment.amount_range`, `payment.budget`, `payment.agent_recurrence`, `payment.allowed_payment_instruments`, `payment.execution_date`. Use the smallest workable `exp`. One open mandate yields one checkout at a time. — [checkout_mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/checkout_mandate.md); [payment_mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/payment_mandate.md)
- [SPEC] Stripe SPT: a single seller, `max_amount`, `expires_at`, revoke at any time, and webhooks on use and deactivation. — [Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/agentic-commerce/concepts/shared-payment-tokens)
- [PRESS] Korean issuers are piloting agent payments (Visa Agentic Ready since 2026-04-30; Shinhan with Mastercard live in March 2026) and target H1 2027 launches. — [Visa Korea](https://www.visakorea.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases/nr-kr-260430.html); [전자신문](https://www.etnews.com/20261023000282)

### Inferences (concrete suggestions)
**A. Delegation levels, mapped to mandate concepts** [INFERENCE]
- **L0 (advice only):** The agent compares and explains and holds no session or credential access. In mandate terms there is no mandate. Output is the comparison artifact only.
- **L1 (default, demo scope; human present, stop before pay):** The agent enters the user's logged-in Coupang or Naver checkout on the user's own phone and builds what AP2 would call the **closed checkout**: the actual final price with coupons, shipping, and payment-method discounts. Then it **stops**. The user's tap on the merchant's pay button, plus the card or pay app's own authentication, plays the role of the **Trusted Surface approving the closed mandate**. Talking point: "L1에서 AI는 결제 권한을 갖지 않는다. 결제 승인은 쇼핑몰과 카드사의 기존 인증 화면에서 사용자가 직접 한다." This matches AP2's own note that human-present flows "can often be replaced with a traditional e-commerce journey."
- **L2 (roadmap; conditional auto-pay within AIR-granted scope):** The user signs an **open mandate** through AIR, which acts as the Trusted Surface and possibly as the Credential Provider. Typed constraints:
  - merchants: `allowed_merchants` = {쿠팡, 네이버 스마트스토어 X}
  - items: `line_items.acceptable_items` = {specific SKUs or options, e.g., the 2 KVM models}, quantity 1
  - **charged-amount cap**: `amount_range.max` = X KRW (final charged amount including shipping and after coupons)
  - window: `execution_date.not_after` = Y, with `exp` set as short as possible
  - instrument: `allowed_payment_instruments` = {the card the user chose}
  - for repeat purchases: `agent_recurrence` + `budget`

  Before acting, the agent re-reads the live checkout and a deterministic checker, **not the LLM**, evaluates every constraint. If anything fails or can't be verified, the agent stops and returns to L1. This is the analog of `unresolved_constraint`.
- Add a **separate, agent-side "effective cost" condition**, for example "청구할인·적립 반영 실질가 ≤ Z원". Label it as checked by the agent and logged, not enforced by the payment network, because networks enforce only the charged amount.

**B. Permission card UI (권한 카드)** [INFERENCE]
- Show the mandate's fields as plain rows: 어디서 (merchants), 무엇을 (exact items/options), 얼마까지 (charged-amount cap, with a note on whether shipping and coupons are included), 언제까지 (expiry date and time), 어떤 결제수단 (card name and last four digits), 몇 번 (one-off or recurring with a total budget).
- Show a "조건 밖이면 → 멈추고 다시 물어봄" row as the default and **unchangeable** behavior.
- Add a prominent **즉시 철회** button (the SPT revoke analog) and a live status: 대기 / 실행됨 / 만료 / 철회.
- Add "AI가 할 수 없는 것" (out of scope). For example: 다른 상품 대체 금지, 수량 변경 금지, 새 카드 등록 금지, 쿠폰/포인트 사용 범위.
- Approval needs biometric or passkey confirmation on a non-agentic screen. An LLM-generated summary alone does not count as consent.
- L1 needs no permission card for payment. Show an "L1: 결제 직전 멈춤" badge plus a session-access consent card (which apps and pages the agent may open).

**C. Audit trail and receipt (결정 영수증)** [INFERENCE]
- For each run, store a tamper-evident record (hash-chained or signed). Include: the constraints the user approved (with their hash), the checkout snapshot the agent saw (screenshot or DOM hash, line items, price breakdown, timestamp), the constraint-by-constraint pass/fail result, the action taken (stopped at L1, or executed at L2), and links to the merchant order number.
- This mirrors AP2's Checkout and Payment Receipts as dispute evidence, and the FTC's "audit trails" point.
- Show the user a readable version ("왜 이걸 이 가격에 샀/멈췄는지").

**D. 4-layer explanation for judges** [INFERENCE]
- **Identity/authority:** the user authenticates with the merchant's own login, the card or pay app's authentication, and a passkey for AIR. For L2, the mandate is a user-signed verifiable credential (SD-JWT in AP2 and Verifiable Intent). Agent identity is presented the TAP/Web Bot Auth way (signed requests, an agent registry) as a roadmap item. A short "selective disclosure" mention suits the Animoca and KSNET judges.
- **AI agent:** non-deterministic, so it only proposes. Deterministic code verifies the constraints. The agent never holds raw card numbers. It runs on the user's device and session.
- **Commerce:** the merchant's live checkout is the source of truth for price, coupons, shipping, and AS terms. The team's agent reads it and does not modify it. Unlike AP2, there is no merchant-signed checkout today, so the agent's snapshot plus a hash serves as evidence. Say this limitation openly.
- **Payment:** L1 uses the user's existing card or pay app with existing 3DS/ISP/app-card authentication, unchanged liability, and normal disputes. L2 uses scoped credentials or tokens via AIR, aligned with the agentic tokens Korean issuers are piloting with Visa and Mastercard for 2027. Stablecoin or x402 can be noted as a pluggable instrument type for digital goods, not the core path.

**E. Claims to avoid** [INFERENCE]
- Do not claim network enforcement of "real personal price." Do not claim AP2 is live in Korea. Do not claim a liability shift exists. Do not imply Amex-style agent purchase protection exists in Korea. Present the H1 2027 issuer timelines as press-reported plans.

### Gaps
- What AIR is and what it can technically sign, hold, or enforce (credential provider? wallet? token issuer?) is unknown to this researcher. The L2 mapping assumes AIR can act as the Trusted Surface and/or Credential Provider.
- Coupang and Naver terms of service on automated agent access to logged-in checkout pages, and any bot-detection or TAP-like agent identification they require, were not researched here.
