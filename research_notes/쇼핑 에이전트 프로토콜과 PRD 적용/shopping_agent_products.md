## Q1. Product-by-product status: launch/expansion dates, merchants and regions, checkout model, confirmation flow

### Takeaway
By Nov 2026 every major platform (OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Perplexity, Amazon) has some way to buy in chat or through an agent. Almost all of them are **US-only, opt-in for merchants (or auto-enrolled), merchant-of-record, and use tokenized wallets (Google Pay, PayPal, Stripe)**, and they ask the user to confirm before purchase. OpenAI **retreated from native Instant Checkout in March 2026** and now focuses on discovery, with checkout handled by the merchant. Google is the most aggressive: UCP in Jan 2026, Universal Cart and AP2 "mandates" for autonomous purchases announced in May 2026. Amazon plays both sides. It runs its own outbound agent (Buy for Me) while suing to keep third-party agents (Perplexity Comet) off Amazon.com.

### Inferences
- [Inference] The checkout models fall into three groups: (1) **protocol-native checkout** where the platform collects payment via a wallet token and the merchant stays merchant of record (Google UCP/AP2, OpenAI ACP, Copilot Checkout, Perplexity–PayPal); (2) **redirect or in-app browser** to the merchant's own checkout (ChatGPT after March 2026, shopping research, Google hand-off); (3) **browser automation** on the user's behalf (Comet, Atlas agent, Amazon Buy for Me on third-party sites). Only group (3) can operate where merchants have *not* integrated, and it is the group attracting lawsuits and security incidents.
- [Inference] OpenAI's March 2026 pullback shows a "native checkout everywhere" model struggling against merchant reluctance. Merchants want to keep checkout because that is where their own pricing logic (loyalty, coupons, upsells) lives.
- [Inference] "Confirmation before purchase" is now table stakes (Google, OpenAI, Atlas). The frontier has moved to **pre-authorized autonomous buying within limits**: AP2 mandates, Amazon auto-buy, Google price-tracked checkout.

## Q2. Does any agent compute a user-specific final price (card benefits, memberships, coupons, points, shipping, tax/customs) and handle stacked conditions?

### Takeaway
**No product publicly documents a cross-store, user-specific, all-in price that stacks card offers, membership/member pricing, personal coupons, points, shipping and tax/customs, and then compares it per candidate × store.** The closest claim is Google's **Universal Cart** (May 2026), which "can factor in payment method perks, loyalty information, and merchant offers" via Google Wallet. That claim is bounded: (a) only Google-integrated merchants, (b) loyalty requires the optional UCP Identity Linking, and (c) Google publishes no stacking logic or per-store breakdown. Every other agent compares **public, logged-out prices** and explicitly disclaims price accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI shopping research "might make mistakes about product details like price and availability" and tells users to check the merchant site. It reads "publicly available retail sites". Personalization comes from memory and preferences, not from the user's accounts. [Primary] — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-shopping-research)
- The Instant Checkout post does not address coupons, loyalty, memberships or tax. Shipping appears only as a confirmation field. [Primary] — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/buy-it-in-chatgpt/)
- ACP carries merchant **promotions** in feeds, while "personalization" is a *future* capability. Loyalty works only inside a single retailer's app (Walmart app with account linking). [Primary] — [OpenAI, Mar 2026](https://openai.com/index/powering-product-discovery-in-chatgpt/)
- Google's Nov 2025 agentic checkout post does not mention loyalty, memberships, card benefits or tax. "Let Google Call" may surface store promos. [Primary] — [Google](https://blog.google/products/shopping/agentic-checkout-holiday-ai-shopping/)
- UCP Identity Linking (OAuth 2.0) carries "loyalty pricing, member discounts, and free shipping" into AI checkout, but only for UCP merchants that implement it, and it is reportedly optional. [Primary + Secondary] — [Google UCP updates](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/shopping/ucp-updates/); [ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/google-ucp-cart-catalog-identity-linking-2026/)
- Universal Cart claims "payment method perks, loyalty information, and merchant offers". The article gives no detail on member pricing, coupon stacking or whether the user confirms. [Primary] — [Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/shopping/google-shopping-cart/)
- Google "Direct Offers" puts **advertiser-funded** discounts (e.g., 20% off) inside AI Mode, so the discount shown depends on who pays for placement. [Press] — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-universal-commerce-protocol-467290)
- Vendor analyses (both with a commercial stake) say agents typically compare the price an anonymous visitor sees, so member, tier and subscriber prices "never enter the comparison". A retailer with steep member pricing therefore looks more expensive to agents than one with no loyalty program. Proposed fix: identity linking. [Secondary] — [Alhena](https://alhena.ai/blog/member-pricing-ai-shopping-agents/); [Seresa](https://seresa.io/seed/ecommerce-analytics/can-ai-agents-see-loyalty-member-pricing); [Voucherify](https://www.voucherify.io/blog/agentic-commerce-optimize-incentives-loyalty-for-ai-agents)
- Amazon auto-buy triggers on Amazon's own price. Buy for Me uses Amazon's payment and address at other retailers, which implies none of the user's loyalty at those retailers. [Secondary] — [Amalytix](https://www.amalytix.com/en/knowledge/ai/amazon-rufus-guide-2026/)
- Affirm surfaces BNPL **total cost** inside AI assistants, one financing dimension of "real price". [Press] — [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/03/04/stripe-affirm-klarna-agentic-commerce/)
- Perplexity–PayPal's launch discount (50% back up to $50) was platform-funded and the same for everyone, not computed per user. [Primary] — [PayPal](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2025-11-PayPal-and-Perplexity-Launch-Instant-Buy)
- Naver's article scenario shows the agent pointing out a membership discount, but only inside Naver's own store. [Press] — [Newsis](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20260206_0003506219)
- Coupon extensions: Honey was alleged to let partner merchants control which codes appear and to replace creators' affiliate cookies (see Q4). That means "auto-applied best coupon" was not necessarily the best available. [Secondary summary of MegaLag allegations] — [Wikipedia, PayPal Honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal_Honey); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/honey-loses-7-000-merchants-as-judge-lets-creator-lawsuit-proceed/)

### Inferences
- [Inference] Industry "real price" efforts are **merchant- or platform-side**. Merchants push their loyalty into a platform (UCP Identity Linking, Walmart app), or the platform pushes its wallet perks (Google Wallet). Neither starts from the buyer's full set of memberships, cards and coupons across competing stores. A buyer-side agent that observes the actual logged-in checkout page avoids that dependency, because the price is read from where the merchant actually computes it.
- [Inference] Stacking is the hard part: store coupon + card instant discount + membership price + points earn/burn + shipping thresholds + per-payment-method conditions. No source shows any agent resolving these interactions or showing a line-by-line breakdown. This remains open white space.
- [Inference] Because platforms disclaim price accuracy, a credible differentiator is **evidence**: a timestamped capture of the actual checkout total and labels such as "confirmed / estimated / unknown". This is a different claim from "lowest price guaranteed".

### Gaps
- No evidence found of any agent handling **taxes/customs (duties)** for cross-border purchases.
- No evidence found of **card-issuer offers** (e.g., Amex Offers, card-linked instant discounts) being used in any agent's comparison, beyond Google's general "payment method perks" line.
- No independent test of Universal Cart's perk and loyalty computation was found.
- No published data on how often logged-in price differs from public price, which would be useful for the pitch. This could be measured in the team's own KVM demo.

## Q3. Agentic shopping adoption metrics (traffic from AI agents, conversion, share of holiday sales), with source and sample

### Takeaway
AI-referred retail traffic grew explosively in the 2025 holiday season (+693% YoY per Adobe) and now converts *better* than other traffic. But Adobe measures **clicks from AI tools**, not agent-completed purchases, and does not publish AI's share of total traffic in these releases. Salesforce's widely quoted "$67B / 20% of orders influenced by AI and agents" includes **retailer-side** recommendations and service bots, not just consumer agents. No source reports the volume of purchases actually completed by third-party agents. 2026 forecasts show sharply slowing growth (+130%). BF/CM 2026 actuals do not exist yet as of 2026-11-22.

## Q4. Documented failures and trust issues: wrong purchases, hallucinated prices, affiliate/ad conflicts, Amazon blocking agents, Amazon v. Perplexity

### Takeaway
The documented record has five strands. (1) **Accuracy**: OpenAI itself admits price and availability errors. (2) **Security**: agentic browsers bought from a fake shop and were prompt-injected into purchases. (3) **Conflicts of interest**: the Honey affiliate case is moving forward in court; platforms are adding ads (ChatGPT ads, Google Direct Offers), merchant fees and checkout-enabled ranking factors. (4) **Liability shifted to consumers**: Target's terms make agent purchases "transactions authorized by you", and there is no agent-specific chargeback code yet. (5) **Platform war over access**: Amazon won and then lost an injunction against Perplexity's Comet, and the case continues.

- [Inference] Prompt-injection incidents argue for **stopping before payment** and for agents that never auto-fill payment credentials. The team's design already does this.

## Q5. Positioning gaps for a user-side, user-context-holding agent vs platform/merchant agents ("whose side is the agent on?"), with implications for the team

### Takeaway
Platform agents are **network-bounded** (only integrated or opt-in merchants), **price-blind to the individual** (public or feed prices; loyalty only via optional merchant linking), **incentive-entangled** (merchant fees, ads, own-store preference) and **mostly US-only**. They push liability to the user while giving thin explanations of *why* a pick was made. The documented white space is an agent that (1) holds the buyer's own context on the buyer's device, (2) reads the **actual checkout total** in the buyer's own logged-in session at each store, (3) shows the stacked breakdown and reasons transparently, and (4) **stops before pay**. In Korea, US checkout agents are not available and Naver's agent is single-platform, so the gap is cross-store (Coupang × Naver) real price.

#### Concrete differentiation points to weave into the PRD and pitch
1. **"최저가는 모두에게 같지만, 진짜 가격은 사람마다 다르다" (the lowest price is the same for everyone, but the real price differs per person) is backed by evidence.** Every major agent compares public or feed prices and disclaims accuracy (OpenAI). Member and loyalty pricing reaches agents only via optional merchant integrations (Google UCP Identity Linking). The team reads the price **where the merchant actually computes it**: the user's own logged-in checkout page, captured with a timestamp. Pitch line: "플랫폼 에이전트는 '공개 가격'을 비교합니다. 저희는 '당신의 결제 화면'을 비교합니다." ("Platform agents compare public prices. We compare *your* checkout screen.")
2. **Show the stack, line by line.** For each candidate × store: list price − store coupon − card instant discount − membership price/benefit + shipping (and points earned shown separately, not netted unless the user chooses). Label each line "확인됨 / 추정 / 미확인" (confirmed / estimated / unverified). No competitor documents stacked-condition breakdowns. This is the clearest open gap (Q2).
3. **"진짜 가격이 제품 선택을 바꾼다" (the real price changes which product you pick) as the demo climax.** Show one case (KVM) where ranking by sticker price differs from ranking by real price for *this* user. Industry data shows AI is mostly a research layer and the human still buys (Adobe clicks, Salesforce "influenced"), so improving the *decision* is the right value claim, not speed.
4. **Declare whose side the agent is on.** Show a persistent "이 에이전트의 수익원: 없음 (광고·제휴링크·판매자 수수료 없음)" (this agent's revenue: none; no ads, affiliate links or seller fees) card. Contrast with the documented Honey affiliate case (MTD denied 2026-06-22), ChatGPT sponsored product ads, Google Direct Offers (paid discounts in AI answers), the Instant Checkout merchant fee and checkout-enabled tie-break factor, and Amazon being both agent and retailer.
5. **Stop before the pay button, with a record (answers "AI를 안심하고 맡길 수 있나요?", "can you safely trust the AI with this?").** Competitors are moving toward pre-authorized autonomous buying (AP2 mandates, Amazon auto-buy), while retailers shift agent-error liability to users (Target ToS 2026-03-22). Agentic browsers have been tricked into buying from fake shops and into prompt-injected purchases (Guardio 2025, SecurityWeek). The team never fills payment credentials, never presses pay, and keeps an on-device log of what was checked and when.
6. **User context stays on the user's device.** Platform "real price" requires linking your accounts into *their* ecosystem (Google Wallet/UCP, Amazon). The team's memberships, card list and coupon context are held locally and used only to read the user's own checkout pages. This is a privacy and "on your side" story.
7. **Understanding beyond a buyer's guide.** OpenAI already does clarifying questions and buyer's guides, and Google does PC-parts compatibility, so "we ask questions and compare" is not unique. Differentiate on the **post-purchase usage environment** (setup outcome, e.g., KVM + monitors + OS behavior; for clothes, fit/occasion; for plants, light/space), **explicit exclusion reasons**, and **linking understanding to real price** in one artifact.
8. **Cross-store in Korea.** US agents' checkout does not operate in Korea. Naver's agent is Naver-only, and no Coupang shopping agent was found. A buyer-side agent spanning Coupang × Naver (and later others) occupies a gap that platform agents structurally won't fill, since each prefers its own store.
9. **Honest claims.** Do not claim "최저가 보장" (lowest price guaranteed) or "최적 보장" (optimal choice guaranteed). Even OpenAI and Target disclaim. Instead say "확인된 조건 기준" (based on verified conditions), show uncertainty labels, and re-check the price at the moment before payment.
10. **Mapping to official criteria.**
    - "AI 에이전트를 써야 하는 이유" (why use an AI agent): N candidates × M stores × K payment conditions is a combinatorial, tedious job requiring logged-in sessions. Humans skip it, and platform agents can't see it.
    - "다시 찾을 이유" (reason to come back): accumulated personal context (memberships, cards, past usage outcomes) makes each comparison more precise, and price re-checks at decision time.
    - "안심" (peace of mind): stop-before-pay, evidence captures, no-ads/no-affiliate disclosure, local data.
